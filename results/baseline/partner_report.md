# Executive Summary of PK/PD Analysis

## Executive Recommendation
Maintain the current dose of 100 mg for the overall population based on robust efficacy evidence, while conducting further validation studies.

## PK/PD Findings
1. **Clearance (CL)** was estimated at **0.380 L/h** with an interquartile range of [0.298, 0.454].
2. **Volume of distribution (V)** was estimated at **4.634 L** with an interquartile range of [3.808, 5.719].
3. Higher exposure (measured as AUC_0-inf) correlates with increased odds of clinical response, notably an **odds ratio (OR) of 2.02** per SD increase.

## Population Insights
- In the upper weight quartile, CL showed a **30.2% increase**, while V increased by **30.8%**. These results suggest potential variability in younger or heavier subgroups, but specific dosing adjustments based on weight require further exploration.
- No adjustments are warranted for patients based on creatinine clearance or mutant allele count, as these factors did not meet the thresholds for clinical significance.

## Dosing Recommendation
- **Overall Population**: **Maintain current dose (100 mg)** due to observed support from efficacy analyses.
- **Upper Weight Quartile**: **No specific dose adjustment** recommended; further exposure-matching studies are required.
- **Creatinine Clearance and Genotype Subgroups**: **No subgroup adjustments** recommended; current dosing remains appropriate.

## Clinical Implementation
Continue with the current dosing regimen while initiating a prospective dose-confirmation study targeted at the upper weight quartile. Implement confirmatory analyses for covariate effects and enhance the detail on safety aspects through larger population PK studies.

## Confidence and Limitations
The analysis predominantly relies on a two-stage approximation rather than a full NLME model, constraining any definitive conclusions about dose proportionality and covariate effects. Diagnostic checks identified issues with predictive performance which necessitate extensive validation efforts to confirm the reliability of these findings before full clinical application.
